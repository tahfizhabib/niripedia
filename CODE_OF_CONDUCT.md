# Code of Conduct

Niripedia is a guide that people use to set up their own computers, and the people who read it and improve it come with very different levels of experience. This code of conduct exists so that everyone can ask questions, report mistakes and send fixes without being put down for it.

## Our pledge

We want taking part in this project to be a good experience for everyone, whatever their age, background, identity, experience, nationality, appearance, religion or the Linux distribution they used before Arch. Maintainers and contributors pledge to be welcoming, patient and respectful.

## What we expect

These things make the project better for everyone:

- Be kind when you answer a question, even one that's already answered in the guide. Point to the section and move on.
- Assume good faith. A confusing step is usually the guide's fault, not the reader's.
- Give feedback on the work, not the person, and say what you'd change.
- Accept feedback on your own contributions gracefully. Nobody gets every command right the first time.
- Credit other people's work, and say where a command or an idea came from.

## What we don't accept

- Insults, name-calling, mocking someone's skill or their choice of tools.
- Harassment of any kind, in public or in private, including repeated unwanted contact.
- Discriminatory jokes, slurs or language aimed at who a person is.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information, such as an address or an email, without their permission.
- Pushing the same argument again and again after a maintainer has given a decision.
- Anything else that a reasonable person would call unprofessional in a shared project.

Strong opinions about window managers, shells and editors are fine. Attacking people over them is not.

## Where this applies

This code of conduct applies in every space the project uses, including issues, pull requests, discussions, comments and any other place where someone represents the project. It also applies when someone is acting for the project in public, such as answering questions on its behalf.

## Reporting a problem

If someone is treating you or another person badly, tell the maintainers. Open an issue that only says you need a private way to report a conduct problem, and leave out the details. A maintainer will reply with a private channel. Reports are handled promptly and fairly, and the maintainers keep the reporter's identity private.

If a maintainer is the person you want to report, say so in the same way and ask for another maintainer.

## What happens next

Maintainers decide what's fair for each case, and they can remove, edit or reject comments, commits, issues and other contributions that don't follow this code. The usual steps, from least to most serious, are:

1. **A correction.** A private or public note saying what was out of line and why, sometimes with an apology requested.
2. **A warning.** A clear statement that the behavior must stop, with the consequences if it continues.
3. **A temporary ban.** A set period with no contact and no participation in the project's spaces.
4. **A permanent ban.** The person is removed for good. This is for serious harm or a pattern of behavior.
